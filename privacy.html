Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 20, 2026 · applies to the MapGauge Chrome extension, Free and Pro plans

MapGauge Free sends nothing to any server. There are no accounts, no analytics and no tracking. Everything the extension does happens inside your own browser, and your audit reports stay on your own computer. With Pro, the exceptions are the licence check with Gumroad and Grid scan, which runs on MapGauge’s own server, both described below.

What MapGauge reads

MapGauge works only on Google Maps pages (https://www.google.com/maps…) and Google Search results pages (https://www.google.com/search…). There it reads the data that Google has already loaded for you:

  • Search results: the businesses in the results list of a search you made, to show badges with their position, category, rating, review count and photo count (with Pro also the quick score, whether the business is claimed, and its website and phone), and the business behind a sponsored result (its category and rating, for a badge marked "Ad"), to compare a business you audit with the top results of that search, to note where a business is in that search (the Rank log, below) and, with Pro, to filter them by website, phone, claimed status and review count (the lead filters). Filter choices are kept only while the Maps tab is open.
  • Google Search: on a search you made, the businesses in the map box and on its "More places" page (name, primary category, rating, review count and Google's IDs for them), to show the same badges. Nothing from Google Search is saved.
  • The business panel: the public details of a business you opened (name, categories, rating, review count, address (also split into street, city, postal code, region and country), map position, website, phone (also in international format), opening hours, photo count, description, attributes such as accessibility or payment options, its booking, reservation and ordering links and its menu link, whether it is a hotel, whether Google shows it as unclaimed, and Google's IDs for it), to build the audit. Also its star breakdown (how many 1★ to 5★ reviews) and, for the few reviews Google loads with the business, only each review's date, star rating and whether the owner replied. MapGauge never uses or keeps reviewers' names, photos or review text. For the owner's newest posts Google loads with the business (up to 10), only each post's date, type (update or event) and button label, never its text, photos or links.
  • The Reviews tab: if you open a business's Reviews tab and scroll it yourself, the reviews Google loads for you there: only each review's date, last edit date and star rating, whether the owner replied and when. MapGauge never uses or keeps reviewers' names, photos or review text, or Google's ID for the review. It shows how many it has next to the Audit profile button. It also notes whether you picked a topic or typed a word in that tab's search box, only to tell that the list is filtered; the topic and the word are never kept.

MapGauge sends no extra requests to Google. It makes no searches you didn't start, doesn't scroll or click for you, and never changes what Google shows. It doesn't read your Google account, your email, your location history or any other website.

While a Maps or Search tab is open, this data is kept only in that tab's memory. It is gone when you close or reload the tab, except what goes into an audit report you save, the Rank log and the self-check's list of which details could be read, all described below.

What MapGauge stores

When you click Audit profile on a business, MapGauge saves an audit report. The toolbar popup shows your most recent reports from the same list. MapGauge also keeps the Rank log described below.

  • What a report holds: the business's public details listed above, the time you saved it, and the address of the Google Maps page you were on (so you can reopen it). That address can include your search words and the map area you were looking at.
  • For the Compare tab: if you opened the business from a search, the report also keeps your search words, the business's position in that search, and for the first 20 other businesses in the results their public name, categories, rating, review count, photo count, opening hours, website, phone, whether it is a hotel, whether Google shows it as unclaimed and its Google ID.
  • For the review audit: if you loaded reviews on the business's Reviews tab before clicking Audit, the report also keeps up to 1,000 of them, each with only the dates, star rating and whether the owner replied listed above.
  • Rank log: where a business appeared in your own Google Maps searches. It is kept for each business you open after a search, and on every search for up to 10 businesses you turn on with "Log this business in my searches" on their report's Rank tab (MapGauge keeps those businesses' Google IDs to know which ones). Each entry holds your search words, the business's position in the results Google had loaded for you (or "not found"), how many results were loaded, when it was first and last seen, and a random ID for that search, under the business's Google ID. MapGauge reads it only from results Google already loaded; it makes no search and sends nothing. At most 50 entries per business and 100 businesses (plus those you turn on); nothing is deleted to make room: when a business's log or the whole log is full, new searches aren't logged until you clear one. Clear log on a business's Rank tab deletes its entries.
  • Last Grid scan (Pro, only if you run one): for each business, its last scan, so its Rank tab shows it again and a scan still running when you closed the page picks up again. It holds the scan's ID, your keyword, the grid size and area, when it started and whether it finished, and for each grid point its map position, the business's position there (or "not in the top 20") and the up to 20 businesses Google Maps showed there (their public name, rating, review count, map position and Google IDs). Only what MapGauge's server sent back. At most 20 businesses; the oldest scan goes first, and a new scan of the same business replaces its last one.
  • Grid scan setup (Pro): the grid size, area and km/mi you chose last, so your next scan starts from them. One setting for all businesses; nothing about the business.
  • Compares used today: the date and how many compares you used on it (Free includes 3 a day). This count stays on your computer.
  • Pro licence (only if you activate Pro): your licence key (also sent with each Grid scan request, see below), whether Gumroad said it is active (or why not, for example "refunded"), and when it was last checked. Removing the key on MapGauge's Pro licence page deletes it.
  • PDF branding (Pro, only if you set it): the logo picture, company name and contact line you enter on MapGauge's Pro page, to print them at the top of your PDF reports. They are never sent anywhere; Clear all on that page deletes them.
  • First-time tip: whether you closed the "Your score is ready" tip on Google Maps, so it isn't shown again.
  • Audit profile bar: whether you collapsed MapGauge's bar at the bottom of the business panel on Google Maps to its small score, so it stays that way.
  • Self-check: for the last 100 search results and the last 100 businesses you opened on Google Maps, only which details MapGauge could read (for example "website: read, phone: read"), and the time the last one was seen. Never a business's name, its ID or your search, so it can't tell which businesses you saw. MapGauge's self-check page uses it to show whether MapGauge still reads Google Maps correctly. It is kept in chrome.storage.session, in memory only, and deleted when Chrome closes or when you click Clear and start again on that page.
  • Where: in chrome.storage.local, the extension's own storage on your computer (the self-check in chrome.storage.session, as above). It isn't synced to your Google account or sent anywhere.
  • How many: at most 50 reports. Auditing the same business again replaces its old report, and the oldest report is removed when a new one would go past 50.

How to delete them: remove the extension. Open chrome://extensions, find MapGauge and click Remove (or right-click the MapGauge icon in the toolbar and choose Remove from Chrome). Chrome then deletes all of MapGauge's saved data. If you install it again, it starts empty. A business's Rank log can also be deleted on its own with Clear log on its Rank tab.

Grid scan and MapGauge's server (Pro)

Grid scan checks where a business ranks on Google Maps across a grid of points around it (5×5 or 7×7). The searches can't run in your browser, so MapGauge's own server runs them for you through DataForSEO, a search data provider. The server runs on Cloudflare Workers at https://api.mapgauge.workers.dev: Cloudflare is the hosting provider that receives and processes these requests for MapGauge, and stores the server's data listed below (Cloudflare's privacy policy). Free never contacts it, and Pro contacts it only for the requests below.

What the extension sends

Like every website, the server sees your IP address with each request. It uses it only for per-minute request limits that protect the service from abuse; it doesn't write it to its database or its logs.

What the server sends on

  • To DataForSEO, once for each grid point: your keyword, the point's map position (with a zoom level), the search language (from the business's country), how many results to return (20), and a label made of the scan's ID and the point's grid position. Not your licence key, your IP address or which business you are checking. DataForSEO runs the Google Maps search and answers with the public results (DataForSEO's privacy policy).
  • To Gumroad, to check that your licence unlocks Pro: your licence key and MapGauge's Pro product ID, the same check the extension makes. The answer is reused for 10 minutes, so most requests don't reach Gumroad (Gumroad's privacy policy).

What the server keeps, and for how long

  • Your licence key: never stored. The server keeps only a SHA-256 hash of it (a fingerprint that can't be turned back into the key) to link your scans and points to your licence.
  • The licence check's answer: whether the licence unlocks Pro (or why not) and when it was checked, under the key's hash. Deleted 30 days after the licence was last used.
  • Scan details, 24 hours: the scan's settings (keyword, map position, grid size, area, country, the business's Google IDs) and each point's position, result state, the business's position there and the up to 20 results DataForSEO returned. The 24 hours let a page that was closed mid-scan pick it up again, and a repeat of the same scan within 24 hours shows the saved answer without using points. An hourly clean-up deletes them after 24 hours.
  • After 24 hours, a scan keeps only its ID, the key's hash, its number of points, its cost and its date, for the hourly scan limit and the cost history. Kept 13 months.
  • Points used: how many points each licence (by its hash) used in each month, for the monthly quota. Kept 13 months.
  • Daily cost: the total DataForSEO cost of each day, with no licence or scan attached, for the daily spending limit. Kept 13 months.
  • Logs: the server's log lines hold only the request's type, its answer code, how long it took, the scan's ID, its number of points and cost, and the first 8 characters of the key's hash, never your licence key, IP address, keyword or map positions. Cloudflare keeps them for at most 7 days. Cloudflare's own per-request logs, which would hold request headers and IP addresses, are turned off.

Your browser keeps its own copy of each business's last scan (see What MapGauge stores). Nobody else can see your scans: each request needs your licence key.

Sharing and selling

MapGauge collects no personal data, and nothing is sold. Your reports, the Rank log and your saved scans stay on your computer, and nobody, including us, can see them. With Grid scan, the server shares only what is listed above with DataForSEO (to run the searches) and Gumroad (to check your licence), and runs on Cloudflare, which processes the requests for MapGauge, and keeps what is listed above for the times given.

The Pro plan

Payments go through Gumroad only, and MapGauge never sees your card details. Pro buttons in the extension open Gumroad's page in a new tab only when you click them; anything you enter there, such as your email, goes to Gumroad under Gumroad's privacy policy.

To unlock Pro you paste the licence key from your Gumroad email on MapGauge's Pro licence page and click Activate. Only then does Chrome ask your permission for MapGauge to contact api.gumroad.com, and the only request is the licence check listed above. Most Pro features use the same Google data described above (all categories, all loaded search results, category gap names, unlimited compares, lead filters); they read nothing more from Google. Grid scan is the one Pro feature that uses a server, as described in Grid scan and MapGauge's server; it includes a monthly number of points (one point is one grid search). The Free plan sends nothing to any server and keeps working without an account.
